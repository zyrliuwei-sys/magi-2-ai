## What the official preview shows

### Character performance

Watch how expression, eye movement, and body language develop together. Check what happens when a character looks away or turns into profile. A consistent silhouette and wardrobe make the performance easier to follow across changing angles.

On a second pass, choose two details you can track, such as the bridge of a pair of glasses and the cuff on a jacket. Compare them before and after a turn. Then watch the hands and eyes separately. A glance that anticipates a gesture can tell you where the character's attention is going before the main action happens.

### Camera movement

Read the first frame before following the camera. Identify the subject and the background lines that establish depth. Then note when the camera starts moving and whether it follows the subject, reveals something new, or ends on a detail.

Use a background object as a reference point. In a sideways tracking shot, a nearby post moves across the frame faster than a distant building. In a pan, the camera can reveal more of the scene while staying in place. That difference helps you choose between instructions like “track alongside the tram” and “pan from the shelter to the tram.”

### Dialogue and sound

Listen for a spoken line while watching the mouth and the listener’s reaction. A pause, a glance, or a change in room tone can mark the end of one beat before the next starts. Watch once with sound and once with captions or a transcript where available.

Replay the moment a speaker finishes a line. Check where the listener is looking, how long the pause lasts, and whether the background sound carries through it. For an action with a clear sound, such as a latch clicking shut, check the point of contact on screen. That gives you a specific timing instruction you can reuse in a prompt.

## How MAGI-2 Preview differs from MAGI-1

MAGI-1 generates video in sequential chunks for streaming and continuation. The August 2026 MAGI-2 Preview is a research release that scales a single model for both picture and sound. It uses a mixture-of-experts architecture to route each part of the input through a subset of the model.

Sand.ai’s November flagship announcement describes a later MAGI-2 model with multimodal reference inputs and more timeline control. Check which release or hosted model a demo uses before comparing it with the research preview.

For MAGI-1, a practical test starts with an existing clip and asks whether the next segment continues the motion. For MAGI-2 Preview, try a scene in which an action and a sound need to happen together, such as a hand setting a glass on a countertop. To test the flagship's reference controls, use a still to establish the subject and a supported video reference to show the camera move. These tests help you judge the capability each release was designed to demonstrate.

### Choose a brief for your first test

Use **Character across a turn** if keeping the face and wardrobe consistent matters most. Choose **A camera move motivated by action** if you want a clear starting cue for the camera. In **Two lines and a pause**, give both speakers time to finish and leave the setting simple so you can hear the timing. If you supply an image for **Product detail in one take**, check whether the product fills so much of the image that the push-in would crop the dial.

## Review the first render

Watch the clip from start to finish without pausing. Note the first moment when the action becomes hard to read. Replay just that section and choose one change to test.

1. **The opening frame is unclear.** Specify the shot size and position of the subject. Try placing a concrete object near the subject to give the frame a point of reference.
2. **The camera starts too early.** Anchor the move to an event the audience can see, such as the tram passing the shelter or the clockmaker closing the watch.
3. **The sound lands at the wrong moment.** Attach it directly to the visible action in the prompt. If speech and a sound effect compete, give one of them a short beat of its own.
4. **The cut changes the subject.** Describe one feature that should carry across the two angles, such as a chipped lid or a folded sleeve.

Keep the original render so you can compare it with the revision. A side-by-side check is easiest when the prompt, reference images, and settings differ in only one deliberate way.
