A MAGI-2 prompt works as a shot brief when you can picture what each sentence changes. Write what the audience sees first, what moves, and where the shot ends. Add a sound cue when timing matters. Start with one short shot so you can tell whether the phrasing worked.

## Describe an opening you could frame

“A cinematic street at night” leaves the subject and camera position open. “Low, wide shot from the edge of a wet curb, with a yellow taxi waiting at the far crosswalk” gives the generator a starting composition. Keep light and weather consistent with the subject. A single reflected shop sign can do more work than a list of colors and lighting terms.

## Give an action its own beat

**Loose brief.** A drummer performs under stage lights while the audience cheers.

**Shot brief.** Hold a close view of the drummer's right hand for the first beat. Pull back as the stick strikes the rim twice. End in a medium shot with the cymbal still vibrating. Let the crowd rise after the second strike.

The revised version tells you what to check. The pullback starts on an action, the second strike arrives before the crowd, and the ending frame shows the cymbal. If the shot cuts away, try “one continuous pullback” and keep the other instructions identical.

## Name what each reference controls

If your MAGI-2 interface accepts multiple references, assign each one a job. A still can set the subject and lighting. A video can demonstrate a camera move. An audio clip can give the rhythm. Describe which aspect to follow, using the reference identifiers shown in that interface.

**Loose brief.** Use the photograph and make the lamp move like the sample.

**Shot brief.** Keep the green shade and brass hinge from the photograph. Use the reference video for the slow sideways camera move. At the end, the lamp remains in the left third, with the light falling onto the page on the right.

## Revise the first thing that breaks

- **The shot opens too close.** Name the opening shot size and where the subject sits in the frame.
- **The camera moves too soon.** Tie the move to an action the viewer can see.
- **The cut loses the subject.** Specify where the subject appears in the last frame before the cut and the first frame after it.
- **Speech overlaps a sound effect.** Give the effect a beat before or after the line.
